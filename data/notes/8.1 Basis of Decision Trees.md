# Basis of Decision Trees

## | 8.1.1 Regression Trees

Imagine that our data is placed in a 2 dimensional space 

( I will use the example of the `Hitters dataset` where we would like to predict the salary knowing the players ~={yellow}Years=~ - years playing in professional League, and ~={blue}Hits=~ - the number of hits in the previous year)

![[Pasted image 20251127083831.png]]

Our predictions we be based on Mean for whole region which makes totally no sense, because of huge difference between inexperienced players and goats.

As we can see the impact of Hits does not affect the players with low Years as much as the players with high value of Years, From that point we can create a cutoff that splits the data so we will achieve a better predictions.

| Process description

1. We start with calculating the RSS for the whole region 
   $$ RSS = (y - \hat{y}) ^2$$
	Where $\hat{y}$ will be our mean for the region
2. Then we start the process to create a new region that will minimize the RSS
	$$RSS = \sum_{j=1}^J \sum_{i\in{R_j}} (y_i - \hat{y_{j}})^2$$
3. Repeat untill some end expression like (untill there will be no region with number of observations > 5)


![[Pasted image 20251127090905.png]]

This so called pruning indicates to the creating the very complex (propably overfitted) tree and in the next steps cut off the branches that don't give a high decrease in RSS.
